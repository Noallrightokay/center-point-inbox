Install

Every installer is on the releases page, with its version in the file name. RATA is in beta and the installers are not code-signed yet, so Windows and macOS ask you to confirm the first time you open it. That is expected.

Windows 10 and 11

Run RATA_<version>_x64-setup.exe.

If Windows says Windows protected your PC, choose More info, then Run anyway.

macOS

RATA_<version>_aarch64.dmg for Apple silicon, RATA_<version>_x64.dmg for Intel. Open it and drag RATA to Applications.

The first time, open RATA from Applications. macOS says it could not check RATA: choose Done. Then open System Settings, choose Privacy & Security, scroll down to the line about RATA, and choose Open Anyway. Confirm when macOS asks again (it may want your password), and RATA opens. After that it opens normally.

If macOS says instead that RATA is damaged and can’t be opened, there is no Open Anyway. Open Terminal and run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/RATA.app, then open RATA again.

Linux

On Debian or Ubuntu: sudo apt install ./RATA_<version>_amd64.deb

On other distributions, make the AppImage executable, then run it: chmod +x RATA_<version>_amd64.AppImage, then ./RATA_<version>_amd64.AppImage

The first time RATA opens it asks for your licence key. Copy it from your licence page and paste it in: one line, no spaces.

Your version is in the window's title bar, as RATA <version> beta. Put it in every bug report.

Link a mailbox

Most providers refuse your normal account password in a mail app like RATA. They want an app password: a separate password, made in your provider's account settings, that only opens mail. Make one for RATA first, using your provider's steps below.

  1. In RATA, open Settings, then Linked accounts, then ＋ Email account.
  2. Enter your email address and the app password, and press Link. It reads Checking… while RATA finds your server and signs in.
  3. RATA works out the server itself. Only if a server field appears, enter your provider's IMAP server name, such as imap.example.com. A pasted imaps://imap.example.com:993 is fine too.

Once it is linked, the mailbox's row in Linked accounts names the provider and server it found, and RATA starts bringing in your mail. The password is kept in your computer's keychain and is never sent to mailrata.org.

Gmail

Gmail app passwords need 2-Step Verification turned on for your Google Account.

  1. Turn on 2-Step Verification, if it is not on already.
  2. Go to myaccount.google.com/apppasswords, give the password a name such as RATA, and create it.
  3. Copy the password Google shows and paste it into RATA with your Gmail address.

If Gmail's settings show an IMAP switch, turn it on. Google explains app passwords in Sign in with app passwords, and IMAP in Add Gmail to another email client.

RATA shows: Gmail · imap.gmail.com

iCloud

For an address ending in @icloud.com, @me.com or @mac.com.

  1. Sign in at account.apple.com.
  2. Open Sign-In and Security, then App-Specific Passwords, and create one for RATA.
  3. Paste it into RATA with your iCloud address.

Apple's own steps: Sign in to apps with your Apple Account using app-specific passwords.

RATA shows: iCloud Mail · imap.mail.me.com

Yahoo

  1. Open your Yahoo Account security page.
  2. Choose Generate app password, name it RATA, and create it.
  3. Paste it into RATA with your Yahoo address.

Yahoo's own steps: Generate and manage 3rd-party app passwords.

RATA shows: Yahoo Mail · imap.mail.yahoo.com

Fastmail

  1. In Fastmail, open Settings, then Privacy & Security, then App passwords.
  2. Create a new app password with access to mail (IMAP and SMTP).
  3. Paste it into RATA with your Fastmail address.

Fastmail's own steps: App passwords. The same works for your own domain when Fastmail hosts its mail.

RATA shows: Fastmail · imap.fastmail.com

Your own domain

For an address like you@yourcompany.com, RATA asks your domain's DNS where its mail is kept, so it finds Google, Microsoft, Fastmail, Hostinger and many other hosts by itself. Use the password your mail host gives mail apps:

  • Google Workspace: an app password from myaccount.google.com/apppasswords, signed in with your work address. It needs 2-Step Verification on, and your administrator may have to allow it.
  • Microsoft 365: no password. See Microsoft below.
  • Fastmail: a Fastmail app password, as above.
  • Another host: the mailbox password or an app password, whichever your host issues for mail apps.

If your company's mail passes through a filter such as Mimecast or Proofpoint, RATA looks at your domain's other public DNS records to find where your mailbox really is, and a Microsoft 365 mailbox is then offered Sign in with Microsoft.

If RATA cannot find the server, it asks for it. Your host's help pages, or your IT administrator, list it as "IMAP server" or "incoming mail server".

Hostinger

Hostinger has no app passwords. Use the mailbox's own password, which you set in hPanel under Emails. Hostinger's own steps: Set up Hostinger Email on your applications and devices.

RATA shows: Hostinger Email · imap.hostinger.com, and the link message says your domain's mail is at Hostinger Email.

Microsoft: Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live and Microsoft 365

Microsoft mailboxes take no password or app password in mail apps. Microsoft lets them in only through its own sign-in page, so RATA has Sign in with Microsoft instead of a password box: it opens Microsoft's page in your browser, and your password goes only to Microsoft.

Not switched on yet. Sign in with Microsoft works only in a RATA build that carries RATA's Microsoft registration. Until a release note says it is switched on, the ＋ Email account form says these mailboxes cannot be added yet. That message is expected.

Once it is on:

  1. Type your Microsoft address in ＋ Email account. RATA shows Sign in with Microsoft where the password box would be.
  2. Press it. Your browser opens Microsoft's page. Choose your account and sign in there.
  3. Come back to RATA. It waits five minutes for you, then stops.

A work or school account may need its IT administrator to allow RATA first. Since late 2025 Microsoft's default settings for organisations no longer let you approve mail access for an app like RATA yourself, so Microsoft's page may say it needs admin approval. Ask your IT administrator to approve RATA for your organisation; a personal Outlook.com, Hotmail or Live address is not affected. If a Microsoft mailbox later needs you again, its row in Settings says Sign in to Microsoft again.

RATA shows: Outlook · outlook.office365.com (or Microsoft 365 for a work address)

When linking fails

RATA says what went wrong in its own words, naming the servers it tried and what each one said. They show in a short message at the bottom of the window, which fades after a few seconds, or on the mailbox's row in Settings, Linked accounts. Below are the sentences you can meet while linking, exactly as RATA says them, with marked parts filled in with your details.

Your password was refused

Gmail rejected the sign-in. Use an app password, not your normal account password — where your provider makes one.
example.com rejected the sign-in. Use an app password, not your normal account password.
What it means
Your provider's server answered and said no to the password. Nearly always this is a normal account password where the provider wants an app password. RATA stops at the first refusal rather than trying the same password elsewhere, because providers count failed sign-ins.
What to do
Make an app password (see your provider) and link again with it. Check you copied all of it.

A linked mailbox stops signing in

you@example.com rejected the sign-in: the app password has probably been revoked. Relink it in Settings → Linked accounts. The server said: the server's words
you@example.com needs relinking — its app password was rejected.
What it means
The app password worked before and no longer does, usually because it was removed in your provider's settings. RATA stops trying that mailbox, so a wrong password is not sent again and again.
What to do
Make a new app password, then link the same address again in Settings, Linked accounts.

Sending is refused

smtp.example.com refused the sign-in for sending. Some providers issue a separate app password for mail apps — regenerate it and relink the account. The server said: the server's words
Could not reach smtp.example.com to send: the reason. Check with your provider that sending from a mail app is enabled for this account.
What it means
Reading mail and sending it use two different servers. The first sentence is the sending server refusing the password; the second is RATA not reaching a sending server at all.
What to do
For the first, make a new app password and link the mailbox again. For the second, check in your provider's settings that sending from mail apps is allowed, and send us the reason it gave.

The server's certificate is not trusted

Could not connect to mail.example.com: mail.example.com could not be trusted: the reason. Check the server address with your mail provider.
What it means
RATA reached the server you typed, but the certificate that proves who it is did not check out, for example invalid peer certificate: UnknownIssuer. Often the name you typed is not one the certificate covers: a host may serve your domain's mail under its own server name. RATA checks certificates the way your computer does, and never connects without that check.
What to do
Use the IMAP server name your mail host gives in its own help pages, not your domain's name, and link again.

No server found

RATA checked example.com’s DNS and tried imap.example.com, mail.example.com without finding a mailbox: the last reason. Enter the IMAP server address below — your provider or IT administrator lists it as "IMAP server" or "incoming mail server".
example.com filters its mail through the filter's server, which does not say where the mailbox itself is. Enter the IMAP server address below — your IT administrator will know it.
What it means
Your domain's DNS did not lead RATA to a mail server that answered, or the domain's mail passes through a filtering service first, which hides where the mailbox is. The last reason, when there is one, says what the last server tried did.
What to do
Type the IMAP server name into the field that appears. Your mail host's help pages list it, or your IT administrator will know it.

The server address you typed does not work

Could not reach imap.example.com. Check the server address with your mail provider.
No mail server answers at imap.example.com.
"what you typed" is not a server address RATA can use. Enter just the name, like imap.example.com.
What it means
Nothing answered at that name, the name does not exist, or what was typed is not a server name at all.
What to do
Check the spelling against your provider's help pages. Type only the name, such as imap.example.com.

RATA will not connect to a local address

mail.example.com is not a public mail server — it points inside a private network, and RATA will not connect there.
"mail.example.com" is not a valid mail server address.
What it means
The server name leads to an address inside a private network, such as your home router, an office network or your own computer. RATA refuses those on purpose, so that a mail server can never point it at the machines around you. The second sentence means the name is not a valid server name at all.
What to do
Use your provider's public server name. A mail server reachable only inside a private network cannot be linked to RATA.

Microsoft needs its own sign-in

Outlook.com, Hotmail and Live mailboxes cannot be added to RATA yet. Microsoft only lets other apps into them through its own sign-in page (OAuth), and no longer accepts passwords or app passwords for IMAP. RATA does not have that sign-in yet.
That address's mail is at Microsoft 365, which RATA cannot open yet. Microsoft only lets other apps into Microsoft 365 mailboxes through its own sign-in page (OAuth), and no longer accepts passwords for IMAP. RATA does not have that sign-in yet.
What it means
Your copy of RATA does not have Sign in with Microsoft switched on yet (see Microsoft). Expected until a release note says otherwise. RATA did not send your password anywhere.
Outlook mailboxes sign in with Microsoft, not with a password, so RATA did not send the password anywhere. Use Sign in with Microsoft.
Microsoft mailboxes sign in with Microsoft instead of an app password. RATA opens Microsoft’s page in your browser, so your password goes only to Microsoft.
What it means
Sign in with Microsoft is on, and the address is a Microsoft mailbox. RATA noticed before sending the password anywhere.
What to do
Press Sign in with Microsoft and sign in on Microsoft's page.
You signed in to Microsoft as other@example.com, which cannot open you@example.com. Sign in as you@example.com, or add other@example.com instead.
Microsoft 365 would not open you@example.com with the sign-in Microsoft gave RATA. A work or school mailbox may have IMAP turned off by its organisation. (the server's words)
Sign in to Microsoft again to keep reading you@example.com.
What it means
The first: you chose a different account on Microsoft's page. The second: Microsoft signed you in, but the mailbox would not open for mail apps, which a work or school organisation can turn off. The third: Microsoft wants you to sign in again, which it asks from time to time.
What to do
Sign in again as the right account. For a work or school mailbox, ask your IT administrator whether IMAP is allowed.

Mail RATA cannot reach

Proton Mail encrypts mail on your device and offers no IMAP server RATA can reach. Their bridge only runs on your own computer.
That domain forwards its mail somewhere else rather than keeping a mailbox of its own. Link the address the mail is forwarded to — that is where it actually lands.
What it means
There is no mailbox RATA can open at that address: Proton Mail and Tuta keep no IMAP server for other apps, and a forwarding service only passes mail on.
What to do
For a forwarded address, link the mailbox the mail is forwarded to.

Report a bug

Open an issue with the Beta bug template on GitHub. It asks for everything below.

  1. The exact error text, copied, not retold. RATA's messages name the servers it tried and what each said, which is what makes a bug quick to find. The message at the bottom of the window fades, so copy it or take a screenshot at once.
  2. Your provider: Gmail, iCloud, a work domain on Google Workspace, and so on.
  3. Your version from the window's title bar, and your operating system.
  4. What you did, what you expected, and what happened.
  5. A screenshot, if the problem is something you see rather than an error.
The issue is public. Never paste:
  • your app password, or any password;
  • your licence key;
  • the contents of RATA's mailboxes.json file, which holds your licence key.

Your own email address is fine to include if you are comfortable with that. If not, write me@ and your provider, such as me@gmail.com: the provider is the useful part.
